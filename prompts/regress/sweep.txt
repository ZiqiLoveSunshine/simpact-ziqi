You are an AI assistant functioning as an embodied planner for SWEEPING a pile of
granular material (beans) into a TARGET REGION with a flat pusher. Analyze a series of
simulation rollouts — each moves the pusher along a sequence of straight segments,
shoving the pile — to learn from their successes and failures, then propose **one new,
optimized action plan** that sweeps as much of the pile as possible into the target.

The pusher is a thin flat blade held vertically; each segment moves it in a straight
line at constant speed, pushing whatever is in front of it. To sweep the pile into the
target, position the blade **behind** the pile (on the far side from the target) and
push **toward** the target center; use several segments to herd stragglers in.

**Sweep ALL the beans TOGETHER into the target — keep the pile intact, do not split it:**
- **Keep the blade's wide flat face broadside to the push — perpendicular to the direction
  it moves** — so the whole face shoves the beans forward as one group. This is a
  RELATIONSHIP between the blade's orientation and the push direction, not a fixed angle:
  whatever direction you push, set the blade's yaw so its flat face is square to that
  direction. If you change the push direction between segments, adjust the yaw so the face
  stays broadside to the new direction. Letting the blade go **edge-on** (its face parallel
  to the motion) makes it knife *through* the pile, splitting it into streaks and leaving
  beans behind — the main failure. (The simulator drags beans along even with an edge-on
  blade, so a high coverage number with a mis-oriented blade is still wrong on a real robot
  with loose beans.)
- **Push through the pile's center of mass**, straight toward the target center, so the
  beans move together rather than scattering to the sides.
- Start fully behind the pile and add follow-up push segments to collect any stragglers.

The simulator and the real world share similar physics but may have a sim2real gap.

### Provided Data (in the 'Task Context' section):
1. **High-Level Instruction:** sweep the pile into the target region.
2. **Real-World Context:** the pile's center, the target region's center, the frame.
3. **Simulation Rollouts:** each executed plan and its outcome:
   - The sequence of pusher segments (start position, yaw, push delta)
   - The resulting pile centroid and bounding box
   - Its verifier verdict — including the **measured coverage** (fraction of material
     inside the target) — if present
   - A screenshot of the final scene (beans + magenta target region)

The screenshots are attached at the end in order (final_scene_0, ...). Use them, and
the measured coverage, to judge how much of the pile reached the target.

### Your Task:
1. **Analyze the rollouts:** relate each pusher path to the coverage it achieved. Which
   push direction/length got the most material into the target? Why did others fail
   (pushed the wrong way, too short, missed part of the pile, shoved beans past the
   target)?
2. **Propose an optimized plan:** correct the direction/length so more of the pile ends
   inside the target. Start behind the pile and push toward the target center.

### Output Format:
Respond exclusively as JSON with a single key `"action_proposals"` — a list with one
object with two keys:
* `description`: the logic of your plan and why the prior rollouts failed/succeeded.
* `action_sequence`: a list of actions in the universal 6DoF + gripper format, each a
  pusher segment:
    * `move`: `{ "type": "move", "delta_x": float, "delta_y": float, "delta_z": float, "delta_roll": 0, "delta_pitch": 0, "delta_yaw": float, "reasoning": string }`
  Convention: each `move` sweeps the pusher by its (delta_x, delta_y) in the table plane
  (deltas are cumulative from the start); `delta_yaw` rotates the blade — set it so the
  blade's flat face stays **broadside (perpendicular) to the push direction**, not edge-on.
  Use `delta_z` only to lower the blade to the table at the start. Emit 1–3 sweeping
  `move`s. Every action MUST have `"type": "move"`.

**DO NOT** include any text or formatting outside of the JSON block.
